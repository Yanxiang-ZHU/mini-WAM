1 · Research question & hypothesis

Modern robotic WAMs/VLAs (e.g. π0.7) generate a visual subgoal with a lightweight world model, then condition a flow-matching action expert on it. This project asks whether that exact World-Model → Visual-Subgoal → Action-Expert structure can be reproduced in a tiny, controlled environment while preserving the information flow and training principle.

Environment2D top-down navigation (WASD), 48×48 grayscale — no colour, so identity is shape×fill only World Modelconditional flow-matching generator of a future visual subgoal (DiT) Action Expertconditional flow-matching generator of an 8-step action chunk (DiT) Controlreceding horizon — predict 8 actions, execute 2, replan Hardwaresingle RTX 5060 Ti, 16 GB VRAM, AMP fp16

3 · The game environment

The environment is deliberately grayscale and colour-free so that object identity can only be recovered from shape × fill geometry — forcing real multimodal grounding instead of a "yellow = target" shortcut. Shapes are area-normalised to remove an "object size" shortcut too.

6 · Answers to the research questions

Q3 — flow matching beats a classifier? YES Action-chunk policy (62.5%) beats simple policy (52.5%) by +10 pts, consistent across OOD splits.
Q1 — visual subgoal helps control? YES A goal-state subgoal (player at target, à la π0 image goals) + a faithfully-rendering world model lifts 62.5% → 92.5% (+30 pts). A short-horizon subgoal is redundant.
Q4 — WAM improves performance? YES Cascade (92.5%) beats the simple policy (52.5%, +40 pts) and is far more efficient (~23 vs ~111 steps).
Q2 — language grounding → compositional generalization? YES With diverse training data, the cascade holds up across unseen combos (90.5%), dense distractors (86%), and multiple obstacles (81%).

7 · Why the subgoal now helps (and what fixed it)

The early world model produced subgoals that were spatially faithful but semantically blurry: shape/fill identity near chance, and a subgoal that was essentially a blurry copy of the current frame. Two changes turned this around:

1 · Goal-state subgoal. The subgoal is now the terminal frame — the player standing at the target — à la π0 image goals, instead of a short-horizon future frame. A short-horizon subgoal is near-redundant with the observation history; a goal state is a strong, informative signal.

2 · Object-weighted loss. Non-background pixels (objects + player) are up-weighted 20× in the flow-matching loss, so the world model learns to render the goal faithfully instead of being dominated by the background. This shrank the real-vs-generated subgoal gap from 7.2 to 1.4 points of action accuracy, taking the cascade from 45% to 71%.
